# Oshurn Sales & Client Conversation Playbook

## Purpose

This playbook captures Oshurn's preferred needs-based insurance conversation. It is a training framework, not a substitute for state law, carrier rules, product training, licensing requirements, or compliance review.

## Core philosophy

**Listen first. Understand the need. Establish affordability. Recommend appropriately. Complete the application accurately. Let underwriting make the risk decision. Stay in service after placement.**

Oshurn should not use pressure, concealment, misleading claims, or coaching a client to omit or alter information.

## Conversation flow

### 1. Professional introduction

Example:

> "Hi, I'm Jeremy with Oshurn Financial. How are you doing today?"

Use Oshurn's approved business identity and communication channels.

### 2. Discovery / revelation question

Preferred opening:

> "What has happened or what's going on that's causing you to look into protection?"

The goal is to understand the client's reason for seeking coverage rather than immediately presenting a product.

### 3. Explore the story

Use open-ended follow-ups:

> "Tell me more about that."

> "What would you want your family to be able to maintain if something happened to you?"

Allow the client to explain their situation in their own words.

### 4. Identify the protection objective

Understand who the client is trying to protect, the financial obligations involved, the desired time horizon, existing coverage, and the client's priorities.

Example:

> "What level of protection would make you feel adequately protected?"

Treat the answer as a starting point for analysis, not a promise that a particular amount or product will be available.

### 5. Establish affordability

Discuss a realistic premium range without creating financial strain.

Example:

> "What would you be comfortable budgeting for this without putting pressure on your normal monthly expenses?"

The objective is appropriate coverage that the client can reasonably maintain. Do not encourage a client to purchase coverage they cannot afford merely to increase production.

### 6. Explain the application and underwriting process

Example:

> "We can review options that fit what you've told me. The application requires accurate information, and the insurance company will determine whether and on what terms it can offer coverage."

Explain that submitting an application is not necessarily the same as the insurer approving or issuing coverage.

### 7. Recommend within authority

Present products that are appropriate to the client's needs, budget, goals, and the producer's actual licensing/appointments.

Explain important product characteristics, costs, limitations, and relevant tradeoffs accurately.

Never represent a product as guaranteed to be approved, suitable, or superior without a factual basis.

### 8. Application accuracy

Review the application carefully with the client. Obtain the information required by the application and carrier underwriting process, including applicable health history, medications, physicians, tobacco/nicotine use, occupation, lifestyle, existing coverage, beneficiaries, financial information, and other requested information.

**Never coach a client to hide, minimize, reword, or omit information to influence underwriting.** If an answer is unclear, obtain clarification from the client, carrier, or BGA rather than guessing.

### 9. Underwriting

Submit complete information through the approved carrier/BGA process. Track requirements and respond promptly to requests.

Underwriting decides whether and on what terms the insurer will accept the risk.

### 10. Policy decision and delivery

Possible outcomes can include approval, modified approval, additional requirements, postponement, or decline. Explain the actual carrier decision and policy terms accurately.

Do not describe an application as a completed policy until the applicable carrier requirements for coverage have been satisfied.

### 11. Long-term client relationship

After placement, Oshurn remains available for appropriate service and periodic review.

Potential touchpoints:

- Policy/service questions
- Beneficiary review
- Life-event check-ins
- Periodic protection review
- Birthday or anniversary communications where appropriate
- Coverage changes when the client's circumstances change

The purpose is service and continued suitability, not unnecessary selling.

## Client trust language

Oshurn can explain that clients can verify the producer's licensing information through the appropriate state resources and can request carrier/product information and required disclosures.

The producer should never imply that licensing itself guarantees a product, outcome, or recommendation.

## Compliance guardrails

- Follow applicable federal and state insurance laws.
- Follow carrier and BGA rules.
- Use only approved advertising and marketing materials.
- Maintain required disclosures and records.
- Do not misrepresent policy benefits, guarantees, exclusions, costs, or underwriting.
- Do not pressure a client to purchase.
- Do not coach answers on applications.
- Do not conceal material information from a carrier.
- Follow applicable replacement rules when existing coverage is involved.
- Verify that the producer is properly licensed and appointed/authorized for the activity and jurisdiction.
- Escalate uncertain product, underwriting, replacement, advertising, or compliance questions to the carrier/BGA/compliance resource before proceeding.

## Oshurn jargon / internal shorthand

**Protection** — Client-friendly term for the financial protection objective; use precise product terminology when discussing the actual policy.

**Revelation question** — An open-ended discovery question designed to uncover why the client is seeking coverage.

**Buy-in** — Evidence that the client understands and agrees that the identified financial risk matters. It should be earned through discovery and education, not pressure.

**Protection number** — The client's desired coverage amount or financial-protection target; it is a starting point for analysis, not a guaranteed recommendation.

**Needs analysis** — The process of understanding the client's dependents, obligations, goals, existing resources/coverage, risk, time horizon, and budget before recommending a solution.

**Budget fit** — Whether the proposed premium is reasonably affordable and sustainable for the client.

**Underwriting-ready** — An application and supporting information are complete and accurate enough to submit through the approved carrier/BGA process.

**Case** — An insurance application once it enters the carrier/BGA workflow.

**Case management** — Tracking outstanding carrier/BGA requirements from application through underwriting and final disposition.

**Placement** — Coverage has reached the applicable carrier-issued/active stage according to the policy and carrier rules; confirm the exact status rather than assuming an application equals placement.

**Persistency** — Continued coverage over time. Strong persistency is better for the client and business than maximizing short-term sales.

**Chargeback** — Commission reversal or repayment that can occur under applicable carrier/contract rules when a policy terminates or otherwise fails to meet the conditions for retained compensation.

**BGA** — Brokerage General Agency; provides carrier/product access, case support, underwriting resources, contracting/appointment support, and other services depending on the relationship.

**Carrier** — The insurance company that underwrites and issues the policy.

**Appointment** — The authorization/appointment relationship required by applicable law and/or carrier rules for a producer to transact certain insurance business on behalf of the carrier.

**E-app** — Electronic insurance application.

**Knockout / decline** — Informal shorthand for an underwriting outcome that prevents the case from proceeding as proposed. Use the carrier's actual decision terminology in client communications.

**Needs-based selling** — Recommend based on the client's documented needs, objectives, financial circumstances, and appropriate product fit rather than simply selling the highest commission product.

## One-line Oshurn sales model

**Lead → Connect → Discover → Explore → Quantify the need → Confirm affordability → Explain process → Recommend appropriately → Apply accurately → Underwrite → Place → Service.**
