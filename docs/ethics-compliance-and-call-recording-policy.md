# Oshurn Financial — Ethics, Compliance & Call Recording Policy

## Purpose

This document establishes Oshurn's internal ethical standard for insurance production and client communications.

It is designed to help the producer protect clients, maintain carrier relationships, protect the insurance license, and create an auditable professional practice.

**This is an internal policy and training document, not legal advice.** Current Texas law, federal law, applicable state law where the client is located, carrier requirements, BGA requirements, and qualified compliance/legal advice control when they differ from this document.

---

# 1. Oshurn's Ethical Promise

> **Tell the truth. Protect the client. Complete the application accurately. Recommend what fits. Never manipulate underwriting. Never misrepresent a product. Never sacrifice compliance for a sale.**

Oshurn is a needs-based boutique practice. The objective is to help clients understand their protection needs and evaluate appropriate insurance solutions.

A commission is never a justification for an unsuitable recommendation, misleading statement, omitted fact, or inaccurate application.

---

# 2. License Protection Rules

The following are non-negotiable internal rules:

1. Never intentionally misrepresent a policy, benefit, premium, guarantee, exclusion, limitation, or underwriting outcome.
2. Never promise that a client will be approved.
3. Never coach an applicant to hide, minimize, alter, or omit information.
4. Never knowingly submit an inaccurate application.
5. Never sign or certify something on a client's behalf unless legally authorized and following the approved process.
6. Never transact insurance outside the producer's actual license, appointment, carrier authority, or state authority.
7. Follow all applicable replacement requirements.
8. Use required disclosures and approved materials.
9. Follow carrier and BGA marketing/compliance instructions.
10. Follow applicable federal and state telemarketing, Do-Not-Call, consent, and communication requirements.
11. Protect nonpublic personal and health information.
12. Maintain required records and make them accessible for legitimate audits/reviews.
13. Escalate uncertainty rather than guessing.
14. Never retaliate against a client who asks questions, declines coverage, requests information, or exercises a legal right.

# 3. Client-First Sales Standard

## We do

- Ask open-ended questions.
- Let the client explain their situation.
- Identify the actual protection need.
- Discuss affordability.
- Explain product characteristics accurately.
- Explain the application and underwriting process.
- Give the client time to understand the recommendation.
- Document material information.
- Follow up professionally.
- Service the client after placement.

## We do not

- Manufacture fear.
- Create fake urgency.
- Pressure a client into buying.
- Misrepresent a carrier or product.
- Hide commission incentives when disclosure is required.
- Tell a client what answer to give on an application.
- Encourage a client to replace existing coverage without following applicable replacement rules.
- Use misleading comparisons.
- Claim a product is "best" without a defensible basis.
- Treat a lead as a guaranteed sale.

# 4. Product Knowledge Rule

If the producer does not understand a product, the producer does not guess.

Use this language:

> "That's a good question. I want to make sure I give you the carrier-accurate answer, so let me verify that before I tell you."

Then contact the appropriate BGA/carrier resource or consult current approved carrier materials.

The BGA/carrier is the authority for current product-specific information, illustrations, underwriting rules, contracting requirements, and approved sales materials.

---

# 5. Underwriting Integrity

Underwriting information must be truthful and complete.

### Never say

> "Don't mention that."

> "Just put no."

> "They probably won't check that."

> "If we leave that off, you'll have a better chance."

### Say instead

> "Let's answer that accurately. If we're unsure how the carrier wants it documented, we'll verify it."

The producer assists with the application; the carrier evaluates the risk.

# 6. Suitability and Needs-Based Recommendation

Before recommending a product, understand the relevant client circumstances and product purpose.

Review, as applicable:

- Protection objective
- Dependents
- Income
- Debts
- Mortgage
- Existing insurance
- Employer coverage
- Financial resources
- Budget
- Time horizon
- Client goals
- Product characteristics
- Alternatives

Do not recommend a product simply because it has a higher commission.

---

# 7. Affordability and Persistency

Oshurn wants business that stays on the books because it continues to serve the client—not business that is sold aggressively and lapses quickly.

Ask:

> "What would you be comfortable budgeting for this without putting pressure on your normal monthly expenses?"

Understand that a policy that is unaffordable can create client harm, lapse risk, dissatisfaction, and potential chargeback consequences under the applicable producer/carrier contract.

---

# 8. Replacement Ethics

Replacing an existing policy can create significant consumer consequences.

When existing coverage is involved:

- Ask the required questions.
- Follow applicable state replacement rules.
- Use required forms/disclosures.
- Do not disparage another policy inaccurately.
- Compare the existing and proposed coverage accurately.
- Do not encourage cancellation of existing coverage before appropriate replacement requirements are satisfied.
- Escalate uncertain cases to the BGA/carrier/compliance resource.

---

# 9. Lead Ethics and Consent

Purchased leads are not automatically compliant simply because a vendor calls them "warm," "exclusive," or "SMS verified."

Before using a purchased lead, retain available documentation concerning:

- Source
- Lead form
- Consent language
- Date/time
- Phone number
- Available consent metadata
- Exclusivity/shared status
- Delivery timestamp
- Vendor compliance representations
- Opt-out process

SMS/OTP verification verifies a phone-number interaction; it should not automatically be treated as proof of every consent requirement applicable to a call or text.

When the legal status of a lead or consent record is uncertain, stop and obtain compliance/legal guidance before contacting the person in a way that requires consent.

# 10. Call Recording Policy

## Oshurn's goal

Oshurn intends to record business calls for legitimate purposes such as:

- Quality assurance
- Training
- Accurate documentation
- Client-service continuity
- Dispute resolution
- Compliance review
- Protecting the client and producer

## Critical rule

**Oshurn will not automatically record every call merely because the technology allows it. Recording must be configured and used in a manner consistent with applicable recording-consent laws, privacy requirements, carrier/BGA requirements, and any stricter law that may apply based on the participants' locations.**

Texas generally permits a party to a communication to record with one-party consent under Texas Penal Code §16.02, but that does not mean every interstate call can safely be recorded without disclosure. Other states may impose stricter all-party/consent requirements. The conservative Oshurn standard is therefore to provide a clear recording notice and obtain the required consent before recording whenever the applicable law or policy requires it. citeturn0search37turn0search6

### Preferred opening disclosure

Use an approved recording notice such as:

> **"This call may be recorded for quality assurance, training, and documentation purposes. Is that okay with you?"**

If the applicable compliance program requires a different notice, use the approved version.

### If the client does not consent

Do not secretly record the call when consent is required.

Use the approved non-recording workflow, such as:

> "No problem. We can continue without recording."

If the system cannot disable recording, stop the recording before continuing or move the conversation to an approved non-recorded channel.

### Interstate calls

Because Oshurn may eventually speak with clients in multiple states, the system should be configured conservatively. Before launch, counsel/compliance should confirm the recording policy for the states in which Oshurn expects to conduct business.

### Automated announcement

Where technically available, use an automatic recording announcement at call start rather than relying solely on memory.

### Recording controls

The call platform should provide, where practical:

- Recording status indicator
- Recording announcement
- Pause/resume recording
- Delete/restrict access controls
- User authentication
- Encryption in transit/at rest where offered
- Audit logs
- Retention settings
- Export capability for legitimate requests

---

# 11. Recording Retention

Oshurn should not keep recordings forever simply because storage is cheap.

Create a written retention schedule after reviewing:

- Texas requirements
- Other states where business is conducted
- Carrier requirements
- BGA requirements
- Insurance record-retention rules
- Litigation/complaint holds
- Privacy requirements
- The purpose for which the recording was created

If a complaint, investigation, audit, litigation matter, or regulatory request exists, the applicable hold takes priority over routine deletion.

Do not assume that a single generic insurance record-retention period applies to every producer, product, state, or record type.

---

# 12. Recording Storage and Access

Call recordings may contain highly sensitive personal information, including financial, medical, identity, and underwriting information.

Therefore:

- Store recordings only in approved business systems.
- Do not store client recordings casually on a personal phone.
- Do not upload recordings to consumer cloud storage without approval.
- Use role-based access where available.
- Do not share recordings casually by text or personal email.
- Do not download recordings unnecessarily.
- Delete recordings according to the approved retention schedule.
- Preserve recordings subject to a legitimate legal/compliance hold.

Texas insurance privacy rules recognize that insurance information can include nonpublic financial and other sensitive information and establish privacy obligations for covered entities. citeturn0search0turn0search11

# 13. Call Recording Training Standard

Every recorded call should be capable of answering:

1. Who was contacted?
2. When did the call occur?
3. Was recording disclosed/consented to as required?
4. What did the client say they needed?
5. What did the producer say?
6. What product information was presented?
7. Were material facts represented accurately?
8. Was the application completed accurately?
9. Was any underwriting advice inappropriate?
10. What was the next action?

The purpose is **accountability and protection**, not surveillance for its own sake.

---

# 14. Documentation Standard

After a substantive client interaction, create an appropriate business record containing:

- Date/time
- Client/prospect identifier
- Reason for contact
- Key needs/objectives discussed
- Material facts relevant to the transaction
- Products/options discussed where appropriate
- Client questions/concerns
- Follow-up action
- Outstanding requirements
- Consent/recording status where relevant

Do not place unnecessary sensitive information in notes.

Use factual language rather than subjective judgments.

Bad:

> "Client was difficult and probably lying."

Better:

> "Client stated they stopped smoking two years ago; application question requires clarification of nicotine history. Follow-up needed before submission."

---

# 15. Communication Ethics

### Phone

Be conversational, professional, and accurate.

### Text

Keep messages concise and compliant. Respect opt-outs and applicable consent requirements.

### Email

Use the Oshurn business identity and approved business account. Avoid unnecessary sensitive information.

### Social media

Do not make unapproved product guarantees, misleading comparisons, exaggerated income claims, or claims about approvals/outcomes.

---

# 16. Conflict-of-Interest Rule

If compensation, carrier incentives, contests, or other financial interests could affect a recommendation, follow applicable disclosure and compliance requirements and keep the recommendation grounded in the client's needs.

Internal question:

> **"Would I still recommend this solution if it paid the same commission as every alternative?"**

If the answer is no, stop and reassess the recommendation.

---

# 17. Client Complaint Procedure

If a client says they were misled, pressured, promised something that did not happen, or otherwise makes a substantive complaint:

1. Listen without arguing.
2. Do not delete or alter records.
3. Preserve relevant communications/recordings.
4. Document the complaint factually.
5. Notify the appropriate BGA/carrier/compliance resource.
6. Obtain legal guidance when appropriate.
7. Do not retaliate against the client.
8. Follow the carrier's complaint/escalation process.

---

# 18. Escalation Rule

When uncertain, use:

> **STOP → DOCUMENT → VERIFY → PROCEED.**

Examples that require escalation:

- Unclear application question
- Unusual underwriting issue
- Replacement situation
- Unclear product guarantee
- Advertising question
- Recording-consent question
- Lead-consent question
- State licensing question
- Client asks for legal/tax advice
- Carrier rule conflicts with a personal assumption

Never guess when the answer could affect the client's coverage, underwriting, compliance, license, or carrier relationship.

---

# 19. Oshurn Zero-Tolerance Behaviors

The following are prohibited as internal business practices:

- Fabricating application information
- Coaching false answers
- Concealing material facts
- Forging signatures
- Misrepresenting a carrier
- Misrepresenting policy guarantees
- Unauthorized recording where prohibited
- Ignoring valid opt-outs
- Using unapproved advertising after being instructed not to
- Deliberately circumventing licensing/appointment requirements
- Deleting evidence after a complaint or investigation begins
- Retaliating against a client

# 20. Producer Self-Check Before Every Application

Ask:

### Client

- Do I understand why they want protection?
- Do I understand who/what they are protecting?
- Do I understand their financial objective?
- Is the proposed premium reasonably affordable?

### Product

- Do I understand this product?
- Is it within my license/appointment authority?
- Can I accurately explain guarantees and non-guarantees?
- Have I explained material limitations?

### Application

- Have I asked every required question?
- Are the answers accurate?
- Did I coach or influence any answer improperly?
- Did I review material answers with the client?

### Compliance

- Are required disclosures complete?
- Does a replacement issue exist?
- Is the lead/contact consent documented as required?
- Is recording being handled lawfully?
- Is the communication channel approved?

### Case

- What is the next step?
- Who owns that step?
- What does the client need to know?
