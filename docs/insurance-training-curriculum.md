# Oshurn Financial — Insurance Training & Producer Development Curriculum

## Purpose

This is the working training curriculum for Oshurn's solo producer. The goal is to build strong product knowledge, needs-analysis skills, ethical sales ability, underwriting competence, case-management discipline, and client-service habits.

This curriculum is a learning framework. It does **not** replace required prelicensing education, continuing education, state insurance law, carrier training, product certification, BGA guidance, compliance review, legal advice, tax advice, or any other required professional training.

## Training philosophy

Oshurn's standard is:

> **Understand the client. Understand the product. Tell the truth. Complete the application accurately. Let underwriting make the risk decision. Protect the client's interests. Build the relationship for the long term.**

We will train through explanation, examples, quizzes, case studies, role-play, objection practice, application simulations, and post-call reviews.

# Module 2 — Product Knowledge

## Objective

Know the products well enough to explain them accurately and compare them appropriately.

### Product areas to study

- Term life insurance
- Whole life
- Universal life
- Indexed universal life
- Final-expense products
- Simplified-issue products
- Guaranteed-issue products where applicable
- Accidental-death products where applicable
- Health/ancillary products only when properly licensed and relevant

### For every product, learn

1. Primary purpose
2. Target client profile
3. How premiums work
4. Death benefit mechanics
5. Cash-value mechanics where applicable
6. Guarantees vs. non-guarantees
7. Common exclusions and limitations
8. Underwriting requirements
9. Suitability/needs considerations
10. Replacement considerations
11. Fees/costs where applicable
12. Policy lapse implications
13. Carrier-specific rules
14. What the product does **not** do

### Golden rule

If you cannot explain a product accurately in plain English, do not sell it yet. Ask the carrier/BGA for training and clarification.

# Module 3 — Needs Analysis

## Objective

Learn to identify the client's actual financial protection problem before discussing a product.

### Discovery areas

- Why the client is looking now
- Who depends on the client
- Income
- Debts and mortgage
- Children/dependents
- Education goals
- Final expenses
- Existing insurance
- Employer coverage
- Business obligations
- Legacy goals
- Time horizon
- Available assets/resources
- Budget
- Client priorities

### Oshurn discovery philosophy

Do not begin with:

> "Which policy do you want?"

Begin with:

> "What has happened or what's going on that's causing you to look into protection?"

Then:

> "Tell me more about that."

The client should have room to explain the problem in their own words.

# Module 4 — Revelation Questions

## Objective

Develop questions that uncover motivation without manipulating the client.

### Core questions

> "What caused you to start looking into protection?"

> "Who are you primarily trying to protect?"

> "What would happen financially if you weren't here?"

> "What would you want your family to be able to maintain?"

> "What concerns you most about that situation?"

> "Tell me more about that."

> "What would make you feel adequately protected?"

### Listening rule

**Ask → listen → clarify → summarize.**

Do not immediately jump to the product.

---

# Module 5 — Buy-In Without Pressure

## Definition

"Buy-in" means the client understands and agrees that the identified risk matters and wants to explore a solution.

Buy-in must be earned through discovery and education.

### Wrong approach

Create fear → manufacture urgency → pressure the client → push the product.

### Oshurn approach

Discover the client's concern → clarify the financial consequence → let the client describe the desired outcome → explain realistic solutions → let the client make an informed decision.

### Key principle

**We do not sell fear. We solve identified protection needs.**

---

# Module 6 — The Protection Number

## Objective

Understand the client's desired amount of protection and translate it into a practical needs analysis.

Suggested question:

> "What number would make you feel adequately protected?"

Then investigate why that number matters.

The client's number is not automatically the recommended amount. Analyze the purpose, obligations, existing coverage/resources, time horizon, affordability, and available products.

---

# Module 7 — Budget and Affordability

## Objective

Avoid recommending coverage that is unrealistic for the client's financial situation.

Suggested question:

> "What would you be comfortable budgeting for this without putting pressure on your normal monthly expenses?"

### Study

- Premium affordability
- Payment frequency
- Client cash flow
- Persistency
- Lapse risk
- Chargebacks
- Premium changes where applicable
- Carrier-specific affordability/suitability considerations

### Principle

A policy that is technically attractive but unaffordable is not a successful recommendation.

---

# Module 8 — Presenting Recommendations

## Objective

Move from discovery to an appropriate product discussion without turning the conversation into a product pitch.

### Transition

> "Based on what you've told me about what you're trying to protect, your timeframe, and what you're comfortable budgeting, I'd like to walk you through the options that appear to fit."

### Recommendation checklist

Explain:

- Why the option fits the stated need
- What it does
- What it costs
- What is guaranteed
- What is not guaranteed
- Important limitations/exclusions
- Underwriting considerations
- What happens next
- Alternatives when appropriate

Never recommend a product solely because it pays more commission.

# Module 9 — Objection Handling

## Objective

Handle objections by understanding them, not overpowering them.

### Common objections

- "It's too expensive."
- "I need to think about it."
- "I need to talk to my spouse."
- "I already have insurance."
- "I don't trust insurance companies."
- "I can get it through work."
- "I don't need it."
- "I want to shop around."
- "I don't want to give you my health information."

### Framework

**Listen → acknowledge → clarify → answer → check understanding.**

Example:

> "I understand. When you say it's too expensive, is the concern the monthly amount itself, or that you're not sure the coverage is worth that amount?"

Never argue with a client. Find the actual objection.

# Module 10 — Underwriting Fundamentals

## Objective

Understand what information the carrier uses to evaluate risk.

### Study areas

- Age
- Medical history
- Diagnoses
- Medications
- Physicians
- Treatment
- Tobacco/nicotine
- Height/weight
- Occupation
- Avocations/hobbies
- Driving history where requested
- Family history where requested
- Financial underwriting where applicable
- Existing coverage
- Replacement information
- Other carrier-specific questions

### Non-negotiable rule

**Never coach an applicant to hide, minimize, alter, or omit information.**

If the producer does not know how to answer a question, obtain clarification from the client, BGA, or carrier.

# Module 11 — Application Mastery

## Objective

Become exceptionally accurate at completing and reviewing applications.

### Practice

We will simulate applications and practice:

- Asking every required question
- Clarifying ambiguous answers
- Reviewing answers with the client
- Identifying missing information
- Collecting supporting information
- Explaining what happens after submission
- Correcting errors promptly

### Producer standard

**The application must tell the truth about the risk being submitted.**

The producer's job is to help the client complete the application accurately, not to engineer an approval.

---

# Module 12 — Case Management

## Objective

Learn what happens after submission.

### Workflow

**Application submitted → carrier review → requirements → producer/BGA response → underwriting → decision → issue/delivery**

### Track

- Application status
- Outstanding requirements
- Medical requirements
- Attending physician statements where requested
- Carrier messages
- Client communications
- Expected next step
- Final disposition

### Service standard

Do not let a case disappear. Every outstanding requirement gets an owner and a next action.

# Module 13 — Policy Issue, Delivery, and Placement

## Objective

Understand the difference between an application, approval, issued policy, and coverage being in force.

Never tell a client that coverage is automatically bound merely because an application was submitted or an appointment occurred.

Use the carrier's actual status and requirements.

### Study

- Conditional receipt rules where applicable
- Initial premium
- Delivery requirements
- Amendments
- Policy acceptance
- Effective date
- Free-look period
- Beneficiary confirmation
- Policy documents

---

# Module 14 — Persistency and Chargebacks

## Objective

Understand why keeping appropriate business in force matters.

### Persistency

Persistency is the continuation of policies over time.

Strong persistency generally indicates that the coverage remains useful and affordable for the client.

### Chargebacks

A chargeback can occur under applicable carrier/producer contract rules when compensation previously paid is reversed or repaid after a policy terminates or fails to satisfy the conditions for retained compensation.

### Principle

**Do not maximize first-year commission at the expense of client affordability and long-term policy value.**

# Module 15 — Ethics and Compliance

## Core rules

- Tell the truth.
- Do not misrepresent benefits.
- Do not hide limitations.
- Do not promise approval.
- Do not coach application answers.
- Do not conceal material facts from the carrier.
- Follow replacement rules.
- Follow advertising rules.
- Follow privacy requirements.
- Follow telemarketing/TCPA requirements.
- Follow Do-Not-Call requirements where applicable.
- Use approved carrier/BGA materials when required.
- Stay within licensing and appointment authority.
- Escalate uncertain situations rather than guessing.

### Red-flag scenarios

Training will include scenarios involving:

- A client asking how to answer a health question to get approved
- A client hiding tobacco use
- A client wanting to replace an existing policy
- A client who cannot comfortably afford the premium
- A lead with questionable consent documentation
- A product that appears to pay more commission but may not fit the need
- A carrier asking for additional underwriting information
- A client asking for tax or legal advice outside the producer's role

# Module 16 — Purchased Lead Compliance

## Objective

Use paid leads responsibly.

Before contacting a purchased lead, understand:

- Lead source
- Submission method
- Consent language
- Date/time of consent
- Phone number submitted
- Available consent record
- Whether the lead is exclusive/shared
- Vendor compliance representations
- Opt-out process
- Applicable federal/state requirements

### Important distinction

**SMS/OTP verification is not automatically the same thing as all legally required telemarketing consent.**

We will not assume a lead is compliant simply because the vendor calls it "SMS verified."

For uncertain legal/compliance questions, escalate to qualified counsel/compliance resources.

# Module 18 — Case Study Training

We will practice realistic client cases.

Example:

> **Prospect:** 42-year-old parent, married, two children, $75,000 income, mortgage, employer coverage, wants to leave financial protection for the family, and has a $100/month comfort range.

Training questions:

1. What is the client's stated reason for seeking protection?
2. What additional information do you need?
3. What financial risks should be explored?
4. What existing coverage should be reviewed?
5. What amount might be worth analyzing?
6. What budget constraints matter?
7. Which product categories might be relevant?
8. What underwriting information must be gathered?
9. What disclosures or replacement questions may apply?
10. What would you tell the client about the next step?

We do not jump directly from the fact pattern to a product recommendation without completing the analysis.

# Module 20 — Professional Communication

Train for three communication modes:

### Phone

Warm, concise, curious, professional.

### Text/SMS

Short, clear, compliant, and appropriate for the consent available.

Professional, useful, and documented.

# Module 24 — Oshurn Professional Script

The producer should not memorize every sentence. Memorize the **structure**.

### Opening

> "Hi, I'm Jeremy with Oshurn Financial. How are you doing today?"

### Revelation

> "What has happened or what's going on that's causing you to look into protection?"

### Explore

> "Tell me more about that."

### Identify the people and risk

> "Who are you primarily trying to protect?"

> "What would happen financially if you weren't here?"

### Protection objective

> "What would make you feel adequately protected?"

### Budget

> "What would you be comfortable budgeting for this without putting pressure on your normal monthly expenses?"

### Transition

> "Based on what you've told me, I'd like to walk you through the options that appear to fit."

### Application

> "The application needs to be accurate because the insurance company uses this information to evaluate the risk. I'll go through it with you carefully, and if we don't know an answer, we'll verify it rather than guess."

### Underwriting

> "Once we submit it, the carrier reviews the information and determines whether it can offer coverage and on what terms."

### Follow-up

> "I'll keep you updated as the case moves through the process and let you know if the carrier needs anything else."

### Service

> "Once everything is issued and in force, Oshurn will remain available for policy questions and appropriate future reviews as your circumstances change."
